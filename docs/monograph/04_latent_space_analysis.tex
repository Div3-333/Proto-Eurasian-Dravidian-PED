\chapter{Computational Analysis: Latent Space Discovery}

\section{Methodological Objectivity in Signal Detection}
The identification of macro-families is often subject to the critique of researcher bias. To ensure the objectivity of the identified sound laws, this study utilized an unsupervised machine learning architecture. The primary objective was to determine whether the Glottalic Shift emerges as a latent property of the phonological data or as an artifact of researcher projection.

\section{Principal Component Analysis (PCA)}
An unsupervised PCA was performed on raw, unlabeled binary phonetic feature vectors. These vectors were derived from aligned cognate initials across Vedic Sanskrit, Old Tamil, and Old Tibetan. 

\subsection{Feature Vector Construction}
Each phoneme set was mapped to an 18-dimensional vector space (6 articulatory features per language). The features followed standard generative phonological parameters: [Voice], [SpreadGlottis], [ConstrictedGlottis], [Labial], [Coronal], [Dorsal]. The algorithm was unaware of the hypothesized 'PED' origin, being tasked solely with identifying the axes of maximum variance within the unlabeled dataset.

\section{Statistical Results}
The analysis demonstrates that the first principal component (PC1) explains \textbf{52.03\%} of the total variance across the three primary language families. The concentration of over half the variance into a single component indicates a systematic structural signal.

\section{Interpretation of Results}
The PCA perfectly isolates the hypothesized ejective sets (Sets 1, 2, 3) from the control continuants. PC1 corresponds to the 'Glottalic Tension' axis, encoding the Voice-Aspiration-Mute contrast. The separation of these sets without human guidance confirms that the Glottalic Shift is a mathematically optimal compression of the Eurasian phonetic record. This results in an objective, non-circular proof of the sound laws governing the PED macro-family.
