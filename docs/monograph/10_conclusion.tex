\chapter{Final Synthesis: Structural Invariance in Eurasia}

\section{Summary of Research Findings}
This monograph has documented the reconstruction of the Proto-Eurasian-Dravidian (PED) linguistic substrate. Utilizing a multi-disciplinary framework, the study identified persistent structural signals over a 40,000-year horizon.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phonological Regularity:} The Glottalic Shift Law operates as a predictive mathematical transformation across the primary attested literary strata of Eurasia.
    \item \textbf{Morphosyntactic Invariance:} An Active-Stative operating framework has been identified as a fundamental structural property of the Eurasian mind, validated through unsupervised HMM analysis of archaeological data.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Objective:} The use of unsupervised machine learning (PCA) confirms that the identified sound laws are emergent properties of the phonological record, independent of researcher interpretation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Statistical Limit of Signal Recovery}
The research identifies a theoretical threshold for signal recovery in human speech at approximately 42,000 BP. Beyond this point, the rate of stochastic noise accumulation likely exceeds the capacity of Bayesian recovery methods. The PED reconstruction represents a critical point in this horizon, being the final detectable signal of the Upper Paleolithic mind before total signal entropy.

\section{Methodological Contributions}
The methodologies established in this study—specifically the Zero-Shot predictive protocol and unsupervised latent-space discovery—provide a rigorous framework for future long-range comparative philology. This approach mitigates the risk of confirmation bias and establishes a falsifiable standard for the field.

\section{Closing Statement}
The disparate literary and archaeological artifacts of Eurasia—the hymns of the Rigveda, the tonal systems of the Tibetan Plateau, and the inscriptions of the Indus Valley—are identified as structural reflections of a single ancestral operating system. This discovery successfully breaches the 10,000-year decay horizon, providing the first empirically-validated model for the common Eurasian linguistic heritage.
