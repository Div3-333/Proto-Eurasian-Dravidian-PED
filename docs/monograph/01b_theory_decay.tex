\chapter{Information Theory and Linguistic Stability}

\section{Stochastic Modeling of Linguistic Evolution}
Linguistic change is characterized by entropic decay. Over time, structural and lexical information is eroded by phonetic reduction and semantic bleaching. This study utilizes an information-theoretic framework to identify specific 'conserved regions' of human language that exhibit high resistance to temporal decay.

\section{Refining the Decay Threshold}
Classic glottochronological models (Swadesh, 1952) utilize a uniform decay rate of approximately 14\% per millennium, which suggests a limit of recognizable signal recovery at roughly 10,000 BP. We hypothesize that the recovery of signals at the PED horizon is contingent upon identifying ultra-conservative structural invariants.

\section{Differential Stability Analysis}
We identify a non-uniform distribution of lexical stability across word classes. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Volatility Strata:} Nouns representing technological artifacts or culturally specific flora/fauna, which exhibit high replacement rates (15–20\% per millennium).
    \item \textbf{Ultra-Conservative Core:} Elements including deictic markers, binary spatial oppositions, and basic physiological verbs. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Monte Carlo Validation: The Conservative Half-Life}
Monte Carlo simulations were executed to model the survival of the core lexicon over 10,000 to 20,000 years. By utilizing a decay rate of 5\% per millennium—supported by empirical studies of ultra-conserved words (Pagel et al., 2013)—the simulation demonstrates that a core skeleton remains statistically recognizable beyond the standard glottochronological limit.

\section{Information Entropy and Signal-to-Noise Ratio}
To ensure methodological rigor, we treat the PED signal recovery as a problem of information theoretic 'Noise Filtering'. We argue that the 10,000-year limit is not an absolute barrier, but a signal-to-noise ratio threshold. By focusing on the high-redundancy structural invariants (e.g., the Active-Stative system), we can detect the ancestral signal through the wreckage of the Holocene transition.
