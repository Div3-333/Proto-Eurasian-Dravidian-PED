\chapter{Phonology: Empirically Derived Invariants}

\section{Transition from Theoretical to Empirical Modeling}
Previous iterations of the PED framework utilized idealized sound laws (e.g., the Glottalic Shift) which, while typologically plausible, failed to account for the full variance of the primary attested data. This chapter presents a new set of phonological invariants derived from an Automated Correspondence Detection (ACD) analysis of real Swadesh datasets for Vedic Sanskrit, Old Tamil, and Old Tibetan.

\section{The Radial Nasal Shift (*N)}
The most widespread and statistically significant signal in the Eurasian substrate is the tripartite divergence of the ancestral nasal.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vedic Node:} Shift to bilabial \textit{*m}.
    \item \textbf{Tamil Node:} Retention of alveolar \textit{*n}.
    \item \textbf{Tibetan Node:} Shift to velar \textit{*ng} or palatal \textit{*ny}.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Dental-Palatal Invariant (*T)}
The ancestral dental invariant governs core topography and relational mathematics. Its divergence reflects the varying degrees of palatalization in the Himalayan and Southern nodes.
\begin{center}
\textit{*T $\rightarrow$ d (Vedic), t/m (Tamil), sh/ny (Tibetan)}
\end{center}

\section{The Velar-Joint Fossil (*K-N)}
The arbitrary mapping of 'joint' or 'bend' to 'perception' (the eye) remains the most diagnostic structural invariant of the PED family.
\begin{center}
\textit{*K-N $\rightarrow$ akṣi (Vedic), kaṇ (Tamil), mig (Tibetan)}
\end{center}
The cluster \textit{*K-N} represents the 'articulating point'. In the Eastern node, the initial velar was lost, while in the South, it was preserved with characteristic retroflexion of the nasal.
