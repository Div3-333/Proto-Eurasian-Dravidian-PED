\chapter{Phonology: The Multimodal Sound Law Engine}

\section{The Glottalic Invariant and Secondary Shifts}
Previous iterations of the PED hypothesis were criticized for using 'idealized' linguistic forms that smoothed over the documented phonetic history of the daughter languages. This study implements a complex, environment-sensitive sound law engine that accounts for secondary developments occurring between the PED horizon (c. 10,000 BP) and the earliest attested literary strata.

\section{Documented Secondary Developments}
To achieve empirical contact with the primary data, the following secondary rules are applied to the PED outputs:

\subsection{The Vedic Node (Indo-Aryan)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Indo-European Vowel Merger:} Ancestral \textit{*e, *o, *a} merge into the single short vowel \textit{a} in the Vedic stratum.
    \item \textbf{The Satem Shift:} Velar stops (\textit{*k, *g}) undergo palatalization to \textit{ś, j} when preceding high front vowels (\textit{*e, *i}).
    \item \textbf{Grassmann's Law:} Dissimilation of aspirates in successive syllables.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Tamil Node (Southern Dravidian)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sibilant Palatalization:} Ancestral fricatives (\textit{*S-}) palatalize to \textit{c-} in initial position before front vowels.
    \item \textbf{Intervocalic Lenition:} Plain stops undergo weakening and voicing in medial positions, though roots typically preserve the initial voiceless quality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Tibetan Node (Eastern Sino-Tibetan)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Palatalization:} Dental and velar onsets undergo palatalization (\textit{*T > sh}) before high front vowels.
    \item \textbf{Prefixation and Coda Loss:} The development of complex prefix systems often obscures the original PED initial, while final codas frequently trigger tonogenesis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Consolidated Empirical Matrix}
By integrating these secondary rules, we can derive the actual attested forms (e.g., Vedic \textit{daśa}) from the hypothesized PED roots (e.g., \textit{*T'eK'-}) without resorting to fabricated 'intermediate' forms.
