\chapter{Phonology: Empirically Derived Invariants}

\section{Transition from Theoretical to Empirical Modeling}
Previous iterations of the PED framework utilized idealized sound laws which failed to account for the full variance of the primary attested data. This chapter presents a set of phonological invariants derived from an Automated Correspondence Detection (ACD) analysis of the full Swadesh-200 datasets for Vedic Sanskrit, Old Tamil, and Old Tibetan.

\section{Methodological Formalization: The Skeletal Alignment Rule (SAR)}
To eliminate the risk of the 'look-alike' fallacy, we implement a formal **Skeletal Alignment Rule (SAR)** for identifying genetic cognates. A tri-language alignment set is only accepted as a PED candidate if it satisfies the following mechanical criteria:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{C1 Alignment:} The initial consonant (Onset) must follow one of the primary systematic radiation laws (Nasal Shift, Dental-Palatal, Labial/Velar Radiation) across all three nodes.
    \item \textbf{C2 Alignment:} At least one subsequent primary consonantal node (Medial or Final Coda) must exhibit systematic phonological correspondence.
    \item \textbf{Position Invariance:} The identified skeletal consonants must appear in the same relative morphological position (Root-Initial or Root-Final) across the aligned forms.
\end{enumerate}

By requiring a multi-node consonantal skeleton, we significantly raise the threshold for statistical coincidence, ensuring that the identified signal reflects a genuine structural persistence rather than superficial phonetic similarity.

\section{The Conditioned Nasal Shift (*N)}
The most widespread signal in the Eurasian substrate is the tripartite divergence of the ancestral nasal.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Western Node (Vedic Bilabialization):} Ancestral \textit{*N} shifts to bilabial \textit{*m} in final agential markers or in the presence of labialized glides (e.g., \textit{*nga > ahám}).
    \item \textbf{Southern Node (Tamil Retention):} Ancestral \textit{*N} is typically retained as alveolar \textit{*n} or palatal \textit{*ñ}.
    \item \textbf{Eastern Node (Tibetan Velarization):} Ancestral \textit{*N} shifts to velar \textit{*ng}.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Dental-Palatal Invariant (*T)}
The ancestral dental invariant governs core topography and relational mathematics.

\section{Labial and Velar Radiation Patterns}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Labial Radiation (*P):} Diverges into Western voiced \textit{b}, Southern voiceless \textit{p}, and Eastern aspirated \textit{ph}.
    \item \textbf{Velar Radiation (*K):} Diverges into Western \textit{k/g}, Southern \textit{k/c} (via palatalization), and Eastern \textit{kh/g}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion: The Empirical Sound Law Engine}
By integrating these conditioned radiation patterns through the Skeletal Alignment Rule, we establish a rigorous framework for deriving the attested Eurasian lexicon. This empirical engine ensures that the identified cognates represent the shared phonological skeleton of the proto-language.
