\chapter{Phonology: Empirically Derived Invariants}

\section{Transition from Theoretical to Empirical Modeling}
Previous iterations of the PED framework utilized idealized sound laws which failed to account for the full variance of the primary attested data. This chapter presents a new set of phonological invariants derived from an Automated Correspondence Detection (ACD) analysis of real Swadesh datasets.

\section{The Radial Nasal Shift (*N)}
The most widespread signal in the Eurasian substrate is the tripartite divergence of the ancestral nasal.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vedic Node:} Shift to bilabial \textit{*m}.
    \item \textbf{Tamil Node:} Retention of alveolar \textit{*n}.
    \item \textbf{Tibetan Node:} Shift to velar \textit{*ng} or palatal \textit{*ny}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Secondary Shift Interaction: The Satem Factor}
The transition from PED to the attested Vedic stratum is mediated by documented Indo-European sound changes. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Satem Shift:} Ancestral palatalized velars (*ḱ) developed into the sibilant \textit{ś} in the Western branch. The PED root for 'ten' (\textit{*T'eK'-}) likely contained a palatalized velar coda which, upon surfacing in the Western node, underwent the standard Satem transformation (e.g., \textit{*déḱmt > daśa}).
    \item \textbf{IE Vowel Merger:} Ancestral \textit{*e, *o, *a} merge into the single vowel \textit{a} in the Vedic hymns.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Velar-Joint Fossil (*K-N)}
The arbitrary mapping of 'joint' or 'bend' to 'perception' (the eye) remains a diagnostic structural invariant.
\begin{center}
\textit{*K-N $\rightarrow$ akṣi (Vedic), kaṇ (Tamil), mig (Tibetan)}
\end{center}
Note that Vedic \textit{akṣi} (from PIE \textit{*h₃ekʷ-}) provides an independent evidence stream that aligns with the PED velar onset, although it has undergone subsequent Indo-European internal development.
