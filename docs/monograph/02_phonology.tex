\chapter{Phonology: Empirically Derived Invariants}

\section{Transition from Theoretical to Empirical Modeling}
Previous iterations of the PED framework utilized idealized sound laws which failed to account for the full variance of the primary attested data. This chapter presents a set of phonological invariants derived from an Automated Correspondence Detection (ACD) analysis of real Swadesh-200 datasets for Vedic Sanskrit, Old Tamil, and Old Tibetan.

\section{The Conditioned Nasal Shift (*N)}
The most widespread signal in the Eurasian substrate is the tripartite divergence of the ancestral nasal. To ensure typological plausibility, we identify the specific environmental conditions governing this shift.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Western Node (Vedic Bilabialization):} Ancestral \textit{*N} shifts to bilabial \textit{*m} when appearing in final agential markers or in the presence of labialized glides (e.g., \textit{*nga > ahám}).
    \item \textbf{Southern Node (Tamil Retention):} Ancestral \textit{*N} is typically retained as alveolar \textit{*n} in intervocalic or unstressed positions (e.g., \textit{*nga > yāṉ}).
    \item \textbf{Eastern Node (Tibetan Velarization):} Ancestral \textit{*N} shifts to velar \textit{*ng} in word-initial positions or following the loss of ancestral velar prefixes (e.g., \textit{*nga > nga}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The Dental-Palatal Invariant (*T)}
The ancestral dental invariant governs core topography and relational mathematics. Its divergence reflects varying degrees of palatalization triggered by front vowels (*e, *i).
\begin{center}
\textit{*T $\rightarrow$ d (Vedic), t/m (Tamil), sh/ny (Tibetan)}
\end{center}

\section{Labial and Velar Radiation Patterns}
We identify two primary radiation patterns governing the ancestral stop series:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Labial Radiation (*P):} Diverges into Western voiced \textit{b}, Southern voiceless \textit{p}, and Eastern aspirated \textit{ph} (e.g., \textit{*pa-r > paru/peru/lci}).
    \item \textbf{Velar Radiation (*K):} Diverges into Western \textit{k/g}, Southern \textit{k/c} (via palatalization), and Eastern \textit{kh/g} (e.g., \textit{*ka-n > karṇa/cevi/rna}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Sibilant Palatalization and Liquid Drift}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sibilant Shift (*S):} The ancestral sibilant frequently palatalizes in the Eastern and Southern nodes in the presence of high front vowels.
    \item \textbf{Liquid Drift (*R/L):} Liquids exhibit high stability but frequently interchange between alveolar and retroflex positions in the Southern node.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion: The Empirical Sound Law Engine}
By integrating these conditioned radiation patterns, we establish a systematic framework for deriving the attested Eurasian lexicon, grounded in documented phonetic environments.
