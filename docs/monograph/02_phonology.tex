\section{The Glottalic Shift Law}
The foundational phonological feature of the PED reconstruction is the hypothesized series of three ejective stops: \textit{*P', *T', *K'}. These sounds are proposed as the unstable precursors to the disparate stop systems found in the daughter languages.

\section{Positional Reflexes of the Laryngeal Invariant (*H)}
The pharyngeal fricative \textit{*H} produced systematic positional effects, referred to as 'phonetic scarring', within the descendant strata.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Compensatory Lengthening:} In the Southern branch, the loss of post-vocalic laryngeals led to the lengthening of preceding vowels (e.g., \textit{*keHṭ-} > Old Tamil \textit{kēṭ-u}).
    \item \textbf{Vowel Coloring:} In the Western branch, the laryngeal influenced the quality of surrounding vowels before its eventual deletion.
    \item \textbf{Tonogenesis:} In the Eastern branch, the loss of final laryngeals provided the phonetic basis for the emergence of lexical tone distinctions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Phonotactic Constraints}
The PED phonological system was characterized by a strict CVC template. Consonant clusters were primarily restricted to morpheme boundaries, facilitating the agglutinative stacking of modular grammatical markers.
