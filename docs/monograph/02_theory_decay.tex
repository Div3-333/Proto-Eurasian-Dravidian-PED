\chapter{Information Theory and Linguistic Decay}

\section{The Glottochronological Event Horizon}
Traditional glottochronology, pioneered by Morris Swadesh, posited that the core vocabulary of a language decays at a constant rate of roughly 14\% per 1,000 years. Simple mathematical extrapolation shows that after 10,000 years, only 20\% of the original vocabulary remains recognizable. After 40,000 years—the timeframe of PED—the remaining signal should be less than 0.05\%, which is statistically indistinguishable from background noise.

\section{The 'Ultra-Conservative' Core Hypothesis}
We challenge the assumption of a uniform decay rate. Human language is not a homogeneous block of information; it is a layered system of varying stability. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Volatility Strata:} Nouns for cultural artifacts (tools, clothing), which shift with every technological change.
    \item \textbf{Low-Volatility Strata:} Binary oppositions (this/that, here/there), basic bodily functions (eat, drink), and pronouns.
\end{itemize}

\section{Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in Philology}
The task of the PED researcher is analogous to 'Signal Recovery' in telecommunications. We do not attempt to reconstruct the whole language (which is impossible); we focus exclusively on the high-SNR frequency bands where the ancient signal is most likely to persist. 

\subsection{The Zipfian Distribution of Exceptions}
Our model accounts for 'Linguistic Noise' by introducing a 15\% exception rate in our sound laws. As proven by our Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (Chapter 6), this 'synthetic noise' perfectly matches the Zipfian distribution of irregularities in natural language. We argue that a reconstruction with 100\% regularity is an artificial forgery, whereas a reconstruction with 15\% systematic irregularity is a biological reality.
