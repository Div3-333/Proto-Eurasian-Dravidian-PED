\chapter*{Preface}
The identification of a common genetic origin for the major language families of Eurasia represents a fundamental objective in comparative linguistics. For decades, the field has operated under the assumption of a 10,000-year limit on signal recovery, beyond which phonetic and lexical drift are hypothesized to obscure all recognizable commonalities. 

This monograph presents a technical documentation of the methodologies and findings that support the existence of a Proto-Eurasian-Dravidian (PED) macro-family. By shifting the analytical focus from superficial lexical correspondences to structural invariants—specifically the Glottalic Sound Law and Active-Stative morphosyntactic alignment—we provide a statistically validated model for the Eurasian linguistic landscape c. 38,000 BP. 

The following chapters detail the application of unsupervised machine learning and zero-shot predictive modeling to primary attested data and the undeciphered archaeological record. This volume is intended to serve as a technical reference for the formalization of deep-time historical linguistics.
