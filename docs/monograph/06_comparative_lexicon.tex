\chapter{Comparative Lexicon: 20 Core Etymologies}

\section{Technical Invariants of the Conserved Core}
This chapter identifies twenty core semantic clusters that exhibit non-linear phonetic alignment across the three primary language families. Every root has been derived using the Sound Law parameters defined in Part II, accounting for documented secondary shifts in the Vedic and Tibetan nodes.

\textbf{*T'ik-} & diś- & tikku & thig & [Indicate/Point]. The dental ejective surfaces as voiced \textit{d} in Vedic. The velar coda \textit{*k} undergoes the Satem shift to \textit{ś} in the Western branch before front vowels. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*K'en-} & aṅga & kaṇ & mkhyen & [Joint/Point]. The velar ejective transformation to Tamil \textit{k} follows the hardening rule. Vedic \textit{aṅga} reflects the nasalized development of the voiced stop reflex. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*M-a-N-} & manas & maṉam & man & [Mind/Stay]. Foundational Interiority root. While Tamil \textit{maṉam} is often categorized as a loan, its integration into the Dravidian stative paradigm suggests a deeper substrate relationship. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*K'oL-} & gala & kuḷal & khol & [Passage/Throat]. Velar ejective mass cluster. The semantic drift from 'concavity' to 'anatomical passage' is consistently attested across the Altai-Dravidian continuum. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*T'ar-} & dāru & tāram & shing & [Tree/Wood]. Dental ejective surfaces as voiced \textit{d} in Vedic. The Tibetan reflex \textit{shing} represents a known palatalization path (\textit{*T > sh}) before ancestral front vowels. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*T'eK'} & daśa & tek- & bcu & [Ten/Many]. The decimal numeral displays the Satem shift (\textit{*k > ś}) in the Western branch. Tibetan \textit{bcu} reflects complex prefixation and coda reduction typical of the Eastern node. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*P'el-} & pela & pāḷ & phel & [Split/Divide]. Labial ejective radiation path. This root provides the basis for the PED partitive system. Tibetan \textit{phel} preserves the ejective-derived aspiration. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*K'em-} & gem- & kam- & kham & [Grasp/Hold]. The velar shift is consistently attested in the earliest literary strata. Semantic stability is maintained as 'binding' or 'compression'. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*es-} & as-ti & (iru-) & way & [To Be]. The stative existential verb. The Western and Eastern branches preserve the \textit{*es-} invariant, while Tamil utilizes a suppletive replacement \textit{iru-}. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*ne} & ne & (al) & na & [Not/Factual]. The alveolar nasal initial is the primary marker of absolute negation across Eurasia. Tamil \textit{al} represents a distal negation innovation. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*mā} & mā & mā & ma & [Not/Prohibitive]. The arbitrary binary negation split. The labial nasal distinguishes the volitional 'must not' from the factual 'is not'. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*ǵenu-} & jann- & kaṇ-tu & kun & [Knee/Angle]. Osteological fossil. This root provides the primary anatomical anchor, linking 'joint' to 'bend' across the radiation. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*bher-} & bhar- & per- & phar & [Carry/Bring]. The labial radiation remains a predictable segment of the divergence, following the $b \rightarrow p \rightarrow ph$ trajectory. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*H-eK} & aham & yāṉ & nga & [Self/I]. Active first-person pronoun. The radial shift from alveolar to labial/velar nasals is a characteristic signature of the PED deictic expansion. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*H-tV} & tvam & (nī) & khyod & [Other/Thou]. Deictic 2nd person. The dental onset governs the mapping of the 'other'. Southern nasalization is a secondary development. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*P'a-} & pitṛ & appa & pha & [Father/Agent]. Primary agential kinship term. Old Tamil \textit{appa} preserves the labial mute with characteristic honorific gemination. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*Ma-} & mātṛ & ammu & ma & [Mother/Source]. Primary stative kinship term. The bilabial nasal is the universal marker of the domestic origin point. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*T'uH-} & udan & tuḷi & thigs & [Water/Drop]. The dental ejective surfaces as voiced \textit{d} in Vedic. Tibetan and Tamil reflexes preserve the 'drop/drizzle' semantic of the original liquid root. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*NaR-} & nṛ & naḻ & nar & [Man/Hero]. The stable alveolar nasal onset denotes the specific identity of the agential human. Vedic \textit{nṛ} preserves the vocalic liquid. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{*S-L-} & sal & uppu & tsal & [Salt/Flow]. Associated with the concept of saline flow. The Old Tamil reflex \textit{uppu} demonstrates the sibilant-to-zero shift followed by vowel prothesis. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]
