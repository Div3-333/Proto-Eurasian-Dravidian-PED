\chapter{Comparative Lexicon: 13 Core Etymologies}

\section{Methodological Culling and Regularity}
To ensure the highest degree of philological sincerity, the PED lexicon has been culled to thirteen core semantic clusters that exhibit statistically significant phonological alignment. We have excluded items prone to horizontal diffusion and those where established family-internal etymologies conflict with the proposed PED root.

\textbf{I (Self)} & ahám & yāṉ & nga & Primary example of the Radial Nasal Shift (*N). Vedic preserves the labial reflex (*m < *n), while Tibetan shifts to the velar (*ng). \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{Thou} & tvám & nī & khyod & Dental-Palatal invariant. The dental onset governs the mapping of the 'other'. Tibetan \textit{khyod} reflects the characteristic palatalization path. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{We} & vayám & nām & nged & Further evidence for the *m/n/ng* triad. The bilabial shift in the Western branch is a diagnostic marker of the Indo-European split. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{Eye/See} & akṣi & kaṇ & mig & The *K-N* Joint Fossil. Note the characteristic retroflexion in the Southern node. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{Two} & dva & iraṇṭu & gnyis & Relational mathematics cluster. The Tibetan reflex \textit{gnyis} preserves the dental-nasal tension (*T > ny) also seen in Tamil \textit{iraṇṭu}. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{To Carry} & bhar- & peṟu & phar & Core activity cluster. Exhibits the Glottalic Shift trajectory ($b \rightarrow p \rightarrow ph$) in its most transparent form. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{This} & idám & itu & 'di & The proximate deictic. The dental invariant remains the most stable anchor of the spatial mapping system. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{That} & tat & atu & de & The distal deictic. Complementary to the proximate set, providing the binary opposition characteristic of the core. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{Not} & na & (alla/il) & ma & Factual negation. The alveolar nasal is the universal invariant. The Tamil development of lateral-initial forms (\textit{alla/il}) is identified as a secondary distal negation innovation specific to the Southern node. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{One} & eka & oṉṟu & gcig & Primary numeral. The velar onset is preserved in the Western and Eastern nodes, with characteristic nasalization in the South. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{Hear/Ear} & kárṇa & cevi & rna & The *K-R-N* auditory cluster. Tibetan \textit{rna} and Vedic \textit{kárṇa} preserve the dental nasal final. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{To Be} & as-ti & (iru-) & yin/yod & The stative existential. Western and Eastern branches preserve the \textit{*es-} invariant, while Tamil utilizes a suppletive replacement. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]

\textbf{To Go} & gam- & cel- & khye- & Primary motion cluster. The velar ejective transformation follows the standard trajectory across the radiation. \\ \addlinespace[12pt]
