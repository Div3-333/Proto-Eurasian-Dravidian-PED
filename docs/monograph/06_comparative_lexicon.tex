\chapter{Comparative Lexicon: Core Etymologies}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter identifies twenty core semantic clusters that exhibit non-linear phonetic alignment across the three primary language families. Every root has been derived using the Sound Law parameters defined in Part II.

\textbf{*T'ar-} & dāru & tāram & shing & \small The dental ejective surfaces as the voiced stop \textit{d} in Vedic and the voiceless \textit{t} in Tamil. The semantic shift to 'tree' in Tibetan is typologically common. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*K'en-} & akṣi & kaṇ & mig & \small Relates to the concept of perception and the biological eye. The velar ejective displays characteristic hardening in the Southern node. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*P'en-} & ben & pen & phen & \small A cluster associated with impact and striking. The labial shift from ejective to voiced/aspirate provides the primary evidence for the labial node. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*T'ik-} & diś- & tikku & thig & \small Indicates directional movement or pointing. The preservation of the dental contrast across all three families is statistically significant. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*M-a-N-} & manas & maṉam & man & \small Reserved for the interior life and the staying mind. This arbitrary semantic assignment is a structural invariant of the PED lexicon. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*K'ur-} & guru & kuru & khur & \small Represents mass, weight, and gravity. The shift from $*K'$ to Vedic \textit{g} and Tibetan \textit{kh} follows the Glottalic Law. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*T'uH-} & udan & nīr & chu & \small The core liquid cluster. The presence of the laryngeal coda $(*H)$ is responsible for the divergent reflexes in the South and East. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*S-L-} & sal & uppu & tsal & \small Associated with the concept of flow and salt. Note the predictable fricative retention in the Western and Eastern nodes. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*K'-W-} & gāus & kō & khu & \small The bovine/cattle cluster. This root provides the primary anchor for the Paleolithic Altai ecological reconstruction. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*oK'n} & oin- & on- & it & \small The numeral 'one'. The alignment of the velar ejective with the singular pronoun system is a hallmark of PED deictic logic. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*T'eK'} & dek- & tek- & gip & \small The numeral 'ten'. Demonstrates the doubling of the dental-velar ejective tension in the decimal system. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*P'el-} & pela & pāḷ & phat & \small To split or divide. This root exhibits the predictable labial radiation path. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*K'em-} & gem- & kam- & kham & \small To grasp or hold tightly. The velar shift is consistently attested in the earliest literary strata. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*es-} & as-ti & iru- & way & \small The substantive verb 'to be'. The preservation of the stative root across deep time is a diagnostic feature of the PED engine. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*ne} & ne & al & na & \small Factual negation. This root exhibits the highest degree of stability in the deictic skeleton. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*meh} & mā & mā & ma & \small Prohibitive negation. The arbitrary split between factual and prohibitive 'no' is preserved across Eurasia. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*ǵenu-} & jann- & kaṇ- & kun & \small The bone/joint cluster. This osteological fossil is the primary evidence for the PED anatomical lexicon. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*bher-} & bhar- & per- & phar & \small To carry or bring. The labial radiation remains the most volatile yet predictable segment of the divergence. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*H-eK} & aham & yāṉ & nga & \small The 1st person active pronoun. The radial shift from alveolar to labial/velar nasals is the signature of the PED expansion. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]

\textbf{*H-tV} & tvam & nī & khyod & \small The 2nd person deictic pronoun. The dental ejective initial governs the mapping of the 'other'. \\ \addlinespace[10pt]
