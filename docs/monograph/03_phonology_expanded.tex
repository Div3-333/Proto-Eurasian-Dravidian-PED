\chapter{Phonology: The Glottalic Architecture}

\section{The Ancestral Ejective Series}
The foundational phonological invariant of PED is the existence of a series of three ejective stops: \textit{*P', *T', *K'}. Mainstream Indo-European studies have traditionally posited a series of 'voiced aspirates' (\textit{*bh, *dh, *gh}), but as Hopper and Gamkrelidze argued, this creates a typologically unstable system. The PED framework recognizes that these were originally harsh, glottalized sounds that softened differently across the Eurasian landmass.

\section{The Dental Matrix (*T')}
The dental ejective \textit{*T'} is the most stable and heavily attested phoneme in our reconstruction. Its divergence follows a strictly deterministic path.

\subsection{PIE (The Voicing Path)}
In the Western node, the PED ejective lost its glottalic pressure, surfacing as the plain voiced stop \textit{*d} in PIE. This is preserved in Vedic \textit{dāru} (wood) and Latin \textit{dent-} (tooth). 

\subsection{Proto-Dravidian (The Hardening Path)}
In the Southern node, the ejective stricture was lost, but the voiceless quality was retained, surfacing as \textit{*t}. In Old Tamil, this often geminates (\textit{*tt}) to mark transitivity, which we interpret as a 'structural memory' of the original ejective burst.

\subsection{Proto-Sino-Tibetan (The Aspiration Path)}
In the Eastern node, the glottalic energy was converted into aspiration, yielding \textit{*th}. This is visible in the Old Tibetan \textit{thig} (mark) and \textit{shing} (wood, via secondary shifts).

\section{The Labial Matrix (*P')}
The bilabial ejective \textit{*P'} presents the highest degree of volatility.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vedic:} Shifts to \textit{*b} (rare in initial position, often lenited to \textit{*bh}).
    \item \textbf{Tamil:} Shifts to \textit{*p}.
    \item \textbf{Tibetan:} Shifts to \textit{*ph}.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Velar Matrix (*K')}
The velar ejective \textit{*K'} governed concepts of mass, sound, and gravity.
\begin{center}
\textit{*K'ur-} (Heavy) $\rightarrow$ Vedic \textit{guru}, Tamil \textit{kuru}, Tibetan \textit{khur}.
\end{center}
