\chapter{Phonology: Sound Law Formalism}

\section{The PED Phonological Inventory}
The foundational phonological feature of the PED reconstruction is the presence of three ejective stops: \textit{*P', *T', *K'}. In contrast to traditional models of Indo-European which posit voiced aspirates, typological evidence suggests that a system based on glottalized stops provides a more stable ancestral state for the Eurasian linguistic substrate. These phonemes underwent systematic, non-linear transformations across the primary geographic nodes of the radiation.

\section{Evolutionary Trajectories of the Dental Ejective (*T')}
The dental ejective \textit{*T'} serves as a diagnostic anchor for the sound laws governing the macro-family. Its divergence follows deterministic trajectories within each major branch.

\subsection{The Western Reflex Node (Vedic Sanskrit)}
In the Western branch, the ejective stricture was lost, surfacing as the plain voiced stop \textit{*d}. This is preserved in the core root for 'wood/tree' (\textit{*T'ar-} > Vedic \textit{dāru}) and the indicatory marker (\textit{*T'ik-} > Vedic \textit{diś-}).

\subsection{The Southern Reflex Node (Old Tamil)}
In the South, the ejective stricture was lost while retaining the voiceless quality, resulting in the reflex \textit{*t}. In Old Tamil, this often manifests as gemination (\textit{*tt}) when marking transitive or causative functions, which is analyzed as a structural remnant of the ancestral glottal burst.

\subsection{The Eastern Reflex Node (Old Tibetan)}
In the East, the glottalic energy was converted into aspiration, yielding the aspirated stop \textit{*th}. This reflex is preserved in Old Tibetan forms such as \textit{thig} (mark) and \textit{shing} (wood, via secondary developments).

\section{Phonotactics and Syllable Structure}
The PED language was characterized by a strict syllable structure, typically following a (C)VC template. Consonant clusters were restricted, occurring primarily at morpheme boundaries during agglutinative stacking.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coda Constraints:} Final stops were rare, typically requiring a sonorant or laryngeal buffer.
    \item \textbf{Vowel Harmony:} A primitive form of height harmony is hypothesized, though its signal is heavily attenuated in the daughter languages.
\end{itemize}
