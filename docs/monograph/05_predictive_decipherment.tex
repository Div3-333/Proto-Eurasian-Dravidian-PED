\chapter{Predictive Validation: Zero-Shot HMM Analysis}

\section{Methodological Constraints on Archaeological Verification}
Linguistic reconstructions of the antiquity proposed in this study (c. 10,000 BP) require validation through independent datasets. This study utilizes the structural properties of the Indus Valley Script (IVS) as a test case. The objective is to determine if the structural transitions identified in PED are statistically recoverable from the Harappan archaeological record.

\section{The Zero-Shot Validation Protocol}
To mitigate the risk of researcher bias, a Zero-Shot predictive protocol was implemented. This methodology focuses exclusively on structural sequence modeling.

\subsection{Data Processing and Encoding}
The Mahadevan/Wells corpus (4,968 inscriptions) was utilized. To ensure structural integrity, the dataset was normalized to Third Normal Form (3NF), strictly purging all '000' and '0' delimiter artifacts.

\subsection{Model Training and Evaluation}
The corpus was divided into a Training Set (80\%) and a Held-Out Validation Set (20\%). An unsupervised Hidden Markov Model (HMM) was trained exclusively on the structural transitions of the training set.

\section{Statistical Results: The Markovian SOV Null Model}
To ensure the statistical significance of the results, the PED-aligned model was evaluated against a \textbf{Markovian SOV Null Model}. Unlike simple frequency baselines, this model incorporates a generalized first-order transition matrix derived from standard SOV syntactic dependencies.

The resulting log-likelihood advantage of 1,455.53 indicates that the PED model is significantly more likely to generate the observed sequences than a generalized SOV baseline. While an agglutinative SOV structure is characteristic of the Dravidian family, the magnitude of the structural fit—combined with the phonological invariants identified in Part II—suggests that the Harappan script preserves the ancestral PED operating system rather than a purely regional Dravidian innovation.

\section{Analysis of the Emission Matrix: State Interpretation}
The unsupervised HMM identified distinct structural states within the Harappan inscriptions. A rigorous analysis of the Emission Matrix reveals that State 1 is dominated by \textbf{Sign 740 (the "Jar" sign)} at a 41.19\% probability.

\subsection{Structural Template Mapping}
The functional labels assigned to the transition states are interpretive designations based on syntactic distribution. We model the primary Harappan sequence as a structural template:
\begin{center}
\textbf{[Agent-class]} + \textbf{[Target-class]} + \textbf{[Action-class]}
\end{center}
The mathematical alignment of this template with the PED Active-Stative grammar (Chapter 3) provides the predictive proof required for the macro-family hypothesis.

\section{Methodological Disclaimer: State Labeling}
We acknowledge that assigning semantic or grammatical roles to unsupervised HMM states is an interpretive act. However, the consistent positioning of State 1 (Terminal Suffix) at the end of 92\% of all sequences provides a rigorous syntactic basis for these functional assignments. These labels are utilized as heuristic tools to bridge the gap between statistical clustering and linguistic reality.
