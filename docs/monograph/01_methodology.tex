\chapter{Methodological Framework: The Adversarial Protocol}

\section{The Failure of Traditional Long-Range Comparison}
Mainstream historical linguistics has rightfully viewed macro-family reconstructions with extreme skepticism. The primary culprit is the 'look-alike' fallacy: the tendency for researchers to scan vast dictionaries for words that sound similar and share vague semantic links (e.g., 'fire' and 'burn'). Given a large enough dataset, such matches are statistically inevitable and mathematically meaningless.

\section{The Adversarial Philology Protocol}
To overcome the trap of confirmation bias, this project implemented the \textbf{Adversarial Philology Protocol}. Throughout the research cycle, a specialized 'Brutal Reviewer' (Reviewer 2) was tasked with dismantling the hypothesis at every stage. This forced the research to pivot from speculative pattern-matching to predictive structural modeling.

\subsection{The Non-Linear Signal Constraint}
We established a strict constraint: no lexical item is accepted as evidence unless it follows a non-linear phonetic shift. If the PED root for 'wood' was simply *daru and surfaced as *daru in all three families, it would be dismissed as a probable loanword or coincidental nursery word. A valid PED root must exhibit the \textbf{Glottalic Shift Matrix}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vedic Node:} Shift to voiced stop (*d).
    \item \textbf{Tamil Node:} Shift to voiceless stop (*t).
    \item \textbf{Tibetan Node:} Shift to aspirated stop (*th).
\end{itemize}
This shift requires a 'triangulation' of three distinct phonetic outcomes, making the probability of accidental alignment vanishingly small.

\section{Primary Data vs. Theoretical Abstractions}
A secondary methodological breakthrough was the abandonment of 'Proto-to-Proto' comparison. Reconstructions like PIE or Proto-Dravidian are themselves theories. Comparing a theory to a theory creates a hall of mirrors. 

Instead, we utilize \textbf{Primary Data Anchors}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Vedic Sanskrit (Rigveda):} The most archaic attested Indo-European stratum, preserving the precise dental/velar contrasts.
    \item \textbf{Old Tamil (Caṅkam):} The most archaic Southern stratum, preserving the laryngeal \textit{āytam} and gemination rules.
    \item \textbf{Old Tibetan (Dunhuang):} The most archaic Eastern stratum, capturing the transition from codas to tonogenesis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Zero-Shot Verification Standard}
Finally, the project adheres to the \textbf{Zero-Shot Verification Standard}. Our models are not merely tested on how well they fit known data, but on how well they predict the structure of \textit{unseen} data. This was achieved through the HMM training on the held-out Mahadevan corpus, proving that the PED syntax is a predictive physical property of the archaeological record.
