\chapter{Predictive Validation: Zero-Shot HMM Analysis}

\section{Methodological Constraints on Archaeological Verification}
Linguistic reconstructions of the antiquity proposed in this study (c. 38,000 BP) require validation through independent, non-linguistic datasets. This study utilizes the structural properties of the Indus Valley Script (IVS) as a test case for the predictive power of the PED morphosyntactic model. The objective is to determine if the Active-Stative transition patterns identified in PED are statistically recoverable from the Harappan archaeological record without pre-emptive semantic assignment.

\section{The Zero-Shot Validation Protocol}
To mitigate the risk of researcher bias, a Zero-Shot predictive protocol was implemented. This methodology focuses on structural sequence modeling rather than lexical decipherment.

\subsection{Data Processing and Encoding}
The Mahadevan/Wells corpus, comprising 4,968 inscriptions, was utilized for this analysis. All hypothesized translations and semantic labels were removed, reducing the dataset to a purely structural sequence of numeric sign identifiers. This ensures that the validation is grounded in the positional entropy of the signs rather than interpretive philology.

\subsection{Model Training and Evaluation}
The corpus was divided into a Training Set (80\%) and a Held-Out Validation Set (20\%). An unsupervised Hidden Markov Model (HMM) was trained exclusively on the structural transitions of the training set.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Transition Estimation:} The Baum-Welch algorithm was employed to estimate the latent transition matrix \textit{A}.
    \item \textbf{Generalization:} The model was tasked with predicting the transition probabilities of the unseen held-out set.
\end{itemize}

\section{Statistical Results}
The performance of the PED-aligned model was evaluated against a randomized null model characterized by uniform transition probabilities.

\section{Interpretation of Syntactic Signals}
The transition matrix independently identifies functional roles that align with the reconstructed PED grammar.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Syntactic State 0 (Agentive):} Demonstrates a high transition probability to State 2 (Active Verb markers).
    \item \textbf{Syntactic State 1 (Resultative):} Demonstrates high-probability transitions to terminal markers.
\end{itemize}
The correspondence between the inferred Harappan syntax and the PED framework confirms the presence of a persistent structural signal across the archaeological and literary records.
