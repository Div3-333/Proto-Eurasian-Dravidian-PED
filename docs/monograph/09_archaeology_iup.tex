\chapter{Archaeological Stratigraphy: The IUP Horizon}

\section{Material Correlates of the PED Radiation}
The temporal reconstruction of the Proto-Eurasian-Dravidian (PED) family necessitates the identification of material evidence for population dispersals during the terminal Pleistocene. This study identifies the \textbf{Initial Upper Paleolithic (IUP)} lithic complex (c. 45,000–35,000 BP) as the material correlate for the proposed radiation. The IUP is characterized by a fundamental shift from flake-based Middle Paleolithic technologies to sophisticated, modular blade and point industries.

\section{The Pamir-Altai Interaction Sphere}
The Pamir-Altai corridor is identified as the geographic hub where the PED linguistic substrate achieved structural maturity. Stratigraphic data from two primary sites provide the evidence for this model:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Kara-Bom (Altai Mountains):} Records a continuous transition from Levallois-like techniques to specialized blade production c. 43,000 BP, representing an early node of technological diversification.
    \item \textbf{Obi-Rakhmat (Tian Shan):} Documents the convergence of distinct lithic traditions, providing a material parallel to the morphological complexity reconstructed for the PED substrate.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Lithic Dispersal Trajectories}
The dispersal of IUP technologies from the Altai-Pamir interface follows three primary axes that align with the linguistic nodes identified in this study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Eastern Axis:} Dispersal into the Tibetan Plateau and the Yellow River basin (e.g., Shuidonggou site, c. 38,000 BP).
    \item \textbf{The Southern Axis:} Movement toward the Indian subcontinent via the Hindu Kush and Khyber Pass interfaces.
    \item \textbf{The Western Axis:} Expansion into the Pontic-Caspian steppe and Eastern Europe (e.g., Bacho Kiro cave, c. 45,000 BP).
\end{itemize}

\section{Technological Modularity and Syntactic Structure}
The emergence of composite tools and modular lithic toolsets during the IUP coincides with the proposed development of the PED modular syntax. We hypothesize that the cognitive shift toward modular, additive assembly in material culture provided the neurological substrate for the highly modular, agglutinative grammar of the PED speakers.

\section{Conclusion}
The alignment of the Glottalic Shift Law with the IUP micro-blade expansion provides a multi-disciplinary validation of the PED radiation. The stratigraphic record of Central Asia confirms the period of ancestral linguistic maturity, providing an empirical anchor for the 40,000-year reconstruction.
