\chapter{Archaeological Stratigraphy: The IUP Horizon}

\section{Material Correlates of the PED Radiation}
The reconstruction of a common linguistic ancestor for Eurasia dating to c. 38,000 BP necessitates the identification of corresponding population dispersals in the terminal Pleistocene archaeological record. This study identifies the \textbf{Initial Upper Paleolithic (IUP)} lithic complex as the material correlate for the PED radiation.

\section{The Altai-Pamir Interaction Sphere}
The Pamir-Altai corridor is identified as the geographic hub where the PED linguistic substrate achieved structural maturity. Stratigraphic data from two primary sites provide the empirical anchors for this model:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Kara-Bom (Altai Mountains):} Excavations here document the transition from Levallois-like Middle Paleolithic techniques to specialized blade production c. 43,000 BP. This site represents the technological node of the ancestral speakers.
    \item \textbf{Obi-Rakhmat (Tian Shan):} Documents the intersection of distinct lithic traditions, providing a parallel to the morphological complexity reconstructed for the PED substrate.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Lithic Dispersal and Linguistic Nodes}
The dispersal of IUP technologies follows three primary geographic trajectories that align with the linguistic nodes identified in this study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Eastern Axis:} Expansion into the Tibetan Plateau and the Yellow River basin (e.g., Shuidonggou site, c. 38,000 BP).
    \item \textbf{The Southern Axis:} Dispersal toward the Indian subcontinent via the Hindu Kush and Khyber Pass interfaces.
    \item \textbf{The Western Axis:} Expansion into the Pontic-Caspian steppe and Eastern Europe (e.g., Bacho Kiro cave, c. 45,000 BP).
\end{itemize}

\section{Cognitive and Structural Invariants}
The emergence of modular lithic toolsets during the IUP coincides with the proposed development of the PED modular syntax. We hypothesize that the cognitive shift toward modular assembly in material culture provided the neurological basis for the highly modular, agglutinative grammar identified in this reconstruction.

\section{Linguistic Palaeontology: Core Ecology}
By mapping stable core roots that follow the Glottalic Shift Law, we can reconstruct the ancestral environment of the IUP horizon. The convergence of three specific conceptual clusters points to a high-altitude, semi-arid interaction sphere.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{*M-L- (Honey/Sweet):} Vedic \textit{melit}, Old Tamil \textit{mel}, Old Tibetan \textit{m-lyar}. The presence of bee-product terminology indicates a temperate highland ecology.
    \item \textbf{*S-L- (Salt/Flow):} Vedic \textit{sal}, Old Tamil \textit{uppu} (replace), Old Tibetan \textit{tsal}. The identification of salt-procurement vocabulary points to the Pamir saline basins.
    \item \textbf{*K'-W- (Bovine/Cattle):} Vedic \textit{gāus}, Old Tamil \textit{kō}, Old Tibetan \textit{khu}. The presence of large ruminant terminology anchors the PED community to the Pleistocene megafauna migration routes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The alignment of the Glottalic Shift Law with the IUP micro-blade expansion and the Altai-Pamir ecological reconstructions provides a multi-disciplinary validation of the PED radiation. The stratigraphic record of Central Asia confirms the period of ancestral linguistic maturity, providing an empirical anchor for the 40,000-year reconstruction.
